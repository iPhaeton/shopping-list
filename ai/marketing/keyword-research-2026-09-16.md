# Keyword research: which searches lead to a shared shopping-list app

Source: Google Ads Keyword Planner, "Discover new keywords", 2026-09-16. Language English,
location "All locations", period Sep 2025 – Aug 2026. Raw export (913 ideas):
[keyword-ideas-2026-09-16.tsv](keyword-ideas-2026-09-16.tsv).

Seeds: shopping list app, grocery list app, shared shopping list, family shopping list,
offline shopping list app, simple grocery list, shopping list app for couples, grocery list app
for families.

## How to read the numbers

- The account has no spend, so volumes are **buckets**, not counts: `50` = 10–100, `500` =
  100–1K, `5000` = 1K–10K, `50000` = 10K–100K searches/month.
- Google groups close variants into one cluster and reports the cluster's numbers on every
  member. Rows with identical volume, competition index and bids (e.g. every "shared/shareable
  … list app" phrasing) are **one** query in practice — do not add them up.
- "Competition" is advertiser competition, not organic difficulty. High competition on a
  phrase like "family grocery list" means content/template sites bid on it, i.e. the searcher
  usually wants a printable list or a budget meal plan, not an app.
- Three-month / YoY change on bucketed data is coarse (jumps like +900% mean "moved up a
  bucket").

## Implications for the name

1. **Search matching comes from the store title's descriptor, not the brand word.** App Store
   and Play titles allow 30 characters; the subtitle another 30. Whatever the brand is, the
   title should read `<Brand>: Shared Shopping List` (or `… Grocery List` in the US listing) so
   that the two clusters with the best volume-to-competition ratio ("shared shopping list app"
   and "shopping list app") match literally.
2. **"Shared" is the word to own.** It has 1K–10K volume, the lowest competition of any
   qualifier, and describes the product truthfully. "Family" pulls in template traffic;
   "offline" and "sync" have almost no demand; "simple" is growing but generic.
3. **Prefer "shopping list" over "grocery list" in the global name**, and put "grocery" in the
   subtitle/keyword field. Volumes are equal, but "shopping list" reads naturally in every
   English market, while "grocery" is American.
4. Keep the brand word short and unlike the incumbents above.
